The scientific starting point

Innate attraction to fermenting fruit runs through two glomeruli, DM1 and VA2 — silencing either one cuts a fly's approach to vinegar. It's yeast on the fruit, not the fruit itself, that draws her in. Odor–food learning involves the mushroom body and dopamine reinforcement.

Semmelhack & Wang, 2009 ↗
Becher et al., 2012 ↗

What is real, and what is not

Real: the projection-neuron → Kenyon-cell wiring. 2,597 Kenyon cells and 139 uniglomerular PNs from FlyWire release 783 (CC BY 4.0), 10,821 connections, a median of about 5 PNs per cell. Which cells connect is measured, not generated.

Also real: odorant → glomerulus responses, from DoOR 2.0 — single-sensillum recordings and calcium imaging, 41 glomeruli × 204 odorants. No structure prediction, no docking. 99 of 139 PNs are driven by measured data; the other 40 innervate glomeruli DoOR does not measure well enough, and stay silent rather than being fed an invented number.

Also real: the value readout. 13,112 measured KC → MBON connections onto 48 output neurons, each one’s pull toward approach derived from which dopaminergic cluster writes to its compartment — PAM carries reward, PPL1 carries punishment. There is no innate-valence table in this model. Nobody tells her what a smell is worth.

Still synthetic: dish recipe proportions and movement rules. Winner-take-all is a uniform top-k stand-in, not the real APL neuron. The circuit panel is schematic, not anatomical, and shows a 180-cell sample of 2,597.

Cherry / banana / citrus are fruit-inspired labels. Their recipes are illustrative fermentation and peel chemistry, not measured headspace of real fruit.

Read the experiment

The lines show the model's response to each isolated odor at a fixed dose, in arbitrary value units. They are not choice probabilities. Visit counters record actual contacts; the phases have unequal durations.

A changed value is learning inside this model, not a measurement of a real fly's feeding preference. “Simulation seconds” are uncalibrated.
